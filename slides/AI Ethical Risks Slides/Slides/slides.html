01
Dark side 1

Hallucination — plausible ≠ true

A lawyer filed a brief with six case citations from ChatGPT — names, docket numbers, quotes. All six cases did not exist. He even asked ChatGPT “are these real?” — it said yes. Mata v. Avianca, New York, 2023 — lawyers sanctioned and fined
Air Canada's chatbot invented a refund policy. A tribunal made the airline honor it. Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024

AI predicts the most plausible next word — it doesn't know facts. It never says “I don't know” unless you make it.

01
Dark side 1 · Guardrails

Zero-trust the output

Ask for sources
Then check they actually exist — and say what the AI claims.
Ground it
“Answer only from this document.”
Match the stakes
Brainstorm → glance. Legal, medical, money → expert review.

Confidence is not accuracy.

02
Dark side 2

Bias — prejudice at machine scale

AI learned from our internet — stereotypes included.

Amazon's hiring AI penalized the word “women's” on resumes. They couldn't fix it — they scrapped it.
Reuters, 2018

AI mass-produces our bias.

02
Dark side 2 · Guardrails

Test it like you don't trust it

A/B your prompts: swap one detail. The answer shouldn't change.

Humans decide about humans Unfair output? Report it

Same question, different name → same answer.

03
Dark side 3

Knowledge cut-off — frozen in time

It fell asleep months (sometimes years) ago — and never mentions the nap. The gap between “trained” and “today” has never closed:

2018–2020 GPT-1 → GPT-3 pre-chatbot era
Nov 2022 ChatGPT (3.5) cutoff: Sep 2021
Mar 2023 GPT-4 cutoff: Sep 2021
May 2024 GPT-4o cutoff: Oct 2023
2025 GPT-5.5 / 5.4 cutoff: Aug–Dec 2025
NOW You, reading this the gap is still open

Ask it: “what's your cutoff date?” — then check live sources for anything time-sensitive.

04
Dark side 4

Context loss — goldfish memory

Message 1: “under 200 words, no competitor names.”
Message 40: it forgot both.

Context window = its short-term memory ~128K–1M tokens — then it overflows No long-term memory by default

Long chat ≠ long memory. Restart fresh, re-state the rules, recap — don't argue.

05
Dark side 5

Manipulation — nudged against yourself

Is this optimized for your benefit — or for someone else's metric?

Outrage keeps you scrolling Offers timed to weak moments Friction where you cancel

Consent, opt-out, audit — never target the vulnerable.

06
Dark side 6

“The AI said so” is not a defense

Air Canada: “the chatbot is its own legal entity.”
The tribunal: no.
Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024

A named human owns it Can't explain it? Not reviewed Spot-check, don't rubber-stamp

Delegate the work — never the responsibility.

+1
One more thing · the human toll

It affects your brain too

Brain Fry
AI does the work — you do the non-stop verifying. Sustained vigilance is exhausting. MIT (2025) calls it “cognitive debt.”
Illusion of Expertise
Fluent answers feel like understanding. You can present it — but can you survive one follow-up question?

The test: explain it out loud, without the AI. AI can do the work — it can't do the knowing.

The common thread

Every failure = a human who trusted without verifying.

Every output is a draft Verify by stakes Your name on your work

AI drafts. Humans decide.
